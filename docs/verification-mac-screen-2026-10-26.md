# This Mac's screen verification

## Implementation

An in-app ScreenCaptureKit display stream excludes Setbeam's application windows, includes the cursor, and sends VideoToolbox H.264 through an explicit stdin pipe to the supervised bundled FFmpeg publisher. The one local relay serves native main/floating monitoring and audience sharing. Display UUIDs are saved through SourceSetup and ProvenSetupStore. See architecture.md for capture dimensions, cadence, bitrate, backpressure, and permission attribution.

## Actual capture after rebase onto Build 12, 2026-10-27

The packaged app was launched directly from a shell that holds Screen Recording access, so macOS attributed capture to that shell. Setbeam's own switch was not changed. Choosing This Mac's screen produced a fresh source 2.5 seconds after preparation and fresh native playback, and the proven-setup gate confirmed it. The experience file reported kind macScreen, sourceAvailable true, nativePreviewPlaying true, sourceHasAudio false.

Exclusion was checked visually. With Setbeam's main window frontmost over other apps on the real screen, its own preview showed the desktop and the other apps' windows without the Setbeam window, so there was no recursive mirror.

Sharing exposed a timestamp defect. The private relay's frames advanced about 11 µs each (one 90 kHz tick), so the 8 fps outgoing thumbnail kept one frame and restarted, and timestamp-paced consumers would misbehave. The same defect reproduced outside the app with VideoToolbox H.264 piped through `-fflags +genpts -f h264 -framerate 60` and stream copy: about 10 µs steps against 16.65 ms with `-use_wallclock_as_timestamps 1`. After the change, the relay's median frame step was 16.69 ms (maximum 17.8 ms) and the audience output's was 16.67 ms. The outgoing thumbnail played steadily. A Chromium-based browser on the Mac's LAN address reported Live feed at 1920×1080 and decoded 60 frames per second. Stop Sharing and Quit left no helper processes or power assertions.

Still unverified: Setbeam's own Screen Recording grant when opened from Finder, floating-preview exclusion on screen, cursor visibility, fine-text quality, displays larger than 1920×1080 (where the bitrate formula reaches about 165 Mbps at 4K60 and the preview decodes in software), display hot-unplug, and long sessions.
